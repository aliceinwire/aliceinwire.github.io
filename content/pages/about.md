### Who I am

I’m **Arisu Tachibana** — kernel hacker, systems builder, community architect.  
I don’t just contribute to open source; I **shape** it. From live patching and CI plumbing to governance and global meetups, I build the tools, policies, and spaces that keep Linux moving.

**What I build.** I created **GKernelCI** (Gentoo’s kernel CI) and the `kci-dev` CLI to integrate with **KernelCI** (KCIDB) at scale, and I built **Elivepatch**, a distributed live kernel patching framework (born from Gentoo GSoC, later presented at Linux Plumbers). I’m also one of the creators of **KTAP v1** (Kernel Test Anything Protocol), and I’ve contributed to upstream tools like **kpatch** (live patching) and **PyPy** (language runtime). I’ve sent patches on **LKML** for more than a decade. I’m listed in MIT’s **xv6** commentary booklet acknowledgments and referenced across university OS labs worldwide.

**How I lead.** I’m the **Gentoo Kernel Project Leader** (see: GLEP-76) and a former **Secretary of the Gentoo Foundation**. I serve as a **Contributing Member of SPI** (Software in the Public Interest), admin **LibrePlanet Japan** & **LibrePlanet Italia**, and a member of the **Community Data Science Collective** research group. I convene study groups, staff booths, and run microconferences—most recently serving as **Primary Leader** of the **Kernel Testing & Dependability** microconference at **Linux Plumbers Conference 2025**.

**Why I stand out.** Long before buzzwords, I was **reading early ecash research** and **playing with ELIZA**—curious about intelligence and trust long before they trended. I’m proudly **gyaru** and a musician (8-string guitar). I mix aesthetics with engineering, because systems—and communities—run best when they’re both rigorous and human.

> *Open source is my practice: build clearly, document loudly, teach generously, and keep the kernel green.*
